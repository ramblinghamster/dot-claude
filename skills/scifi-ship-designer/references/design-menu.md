# Sci-Fi Fighter/Ship Design Menu
*A vehicle-designer's spec sheet — pick one value per line, combine freely.*

---

## 0. Size Class & Role Archetype
*Pick this first — it sets the default "gravity" for every category below. You can still override any individual value, but this tells you where to start.*

**Size class**
- **Small** (snub craft, single/dual-seat fighters, racers, light shuttles — roughly 5–20m)
- **Medium** (gunships, freighters, multi-crew explorers, dropships — roughly 20–60m)
- **Heavy** (corvette/frigate-adjacent, carriers, capital escorts — 60m+)

**Role archetype**
- Racer/interceptor (speed prioritized above all else)
- Dogfighter (agility and weapons balance)
- Gunship/bomber (heavy weapons, armor over agility)
- Hauler/freighter (cargo volume prioritized, minimal combat features)
- Explorer/scout (sensor and fuel range prioritized, light weapons)
- Dropship/troop transport (crew capacity + cargo ramp, moderate armor)
- Capital escort/corvette (heavy armor + point-defense weapons, crewed bridge)

**Typical tendencies by size class** *(defaults, not rules — override freely)*
- Small → integral or podded engines, flush weapons, single hull segment, minimal crew signaling
- Medium → podded or bolted engines, mixed weapon mounting, 2–3 hull segments, some visible crew/cargo features
- Heavy → bolted or distributed engines, turreted/heavy weapons, many hull segments, prominent crew/cargo signaling (windows, walkways, docking collars)

## 1. Scale & Mass

**Overall length**
- Single-seat (fighter/interceptor scale)
- Two-seat (heavy fighter/gunship scale)
- Small multi-role (shuttle/light dropship scale)
- Medium multi-crew (freighter/explorer scale)
- Heavy/capital-adjacent (corvette/frigate/carrier scale)

*These pair naturally with the size classes in category 0 (single-seat/two-seat → Small,
small/medium multi-role → Medium, heavy/capital-adjacent → Heavy) but aren't a strict lookup
table — a Medium-role gunship with a pilot+gunner crew can reasonably sit at "Two-seat" length
even though its size class is Medium. Pick whichever length best matches the crew count and
role from category 0, and don't let a mismatch with the size class label alone block a choice.*

**Mass distribution**
- Nose-heavy (bulky forward sensor/weapons block, slender tail)
- Rear-heavy (large engine block dominates, slim forward hull)
- Centered (mass concentrated around cockpit/mid-hull, tapered both ends)
- Evenly distributed (uniform cross-section, no dominant mass zone)
- Segmented mass blocks (distinct heavy zones per hull segment — common at heavy scale: forward bridge block, mid cargo/reactor block, aft engine block, each reading as its own mass)

**Crew count**
- Single pilot (small, tight canopy)
- Pilot + gunner (elongated canopy or twin bubble)
- Small crew, 3–5 (enclosed bridge/cockpit block, no exterior canopy)
- Working crew, 6–15 (dedicated bridge structure, separate crew/cargo zones, visible external walkways or catwalks)
- Large crew, 15+ (multi-deck superstructure, bridge tower or command spine distinct from main hull mass)

## 2. Hull Geometry

**Primary hull shape**
- Wedge (tapering from wide rear to narrow nose)
- Slab (flat rectangular block, minimal taper)
- Cylinder (rounded tube-like body)
- Ovoid (rounded, egg-like body)
- Faceted polyhedron (angular, multi-plane block)

**Surface treatment**
- Hard faceted planes (stealth-aircraft style, angular reflective panels)
- Smooth continuous curves (organic, hydrodynamic-looking despite vacuum use)
- Mixed (faceted hull with rounded transitions at cockpit/engine junctions)

**Symmetry**
- Bilateral symmetric (mirrored left/right)
- Asymmetric offset cockpit (canopy pushed to one side)
- Asymmetric pod placement (uneven left/right equipment pods)

**Hull segmentation**
- Fused/seamless (nose-to-tail reads as one continuous mass)
- Clearly segmented (visible nose block, mid-body, aft block with distinct panel breaks)
- Modular (hull built from visibly bolted-together sections, like shipping containers)
- Spine-and-rib (heavy scale: a long central spine/keel structure with hull sections and equipment hung off it — superstructure reads as built around an internal frame rather than solid mass)
- Layered decks (heavy scale: hull built as visibly stacked horizontal deck bands, like a ship's hull plating rows)

---

## 3. Cockpit / Canopy / Bridge
*At small/medium scale this is a cockpit or canopy; at heavy scale it becomes a bridge structure — same design questions apply at a different scale.*

**Position**
- Flush-mounted (canopy sits level with hull surface)
- Raised on a neck/spine (canopy elevated above main hull line)
- Fully internal (no canopy — cameras/sensors only, armored hull unbroken)
- Elevated bridge tower (heavy scale: distinct command structure raised above the main hull mass, like a ship's superstructure)
- Forward bridge block (heavy scale: bridge integrated into the leading edge of the hull rather than raised — flush with the bow)

**Visibility profile**
- Bubble canopy (large curved transparent dome)
- Slit viewport (narrow horizontal window band)
- No direct viewport (sensor-fed internal display only)
- Wraparound bridge windows (heavy scale: wide multi-panel viewport band across a bridge structure)
- Armored bridge slit (heavy scale: minimal narrow windows, primarily sensor-reliant, reads as heavily protected)

**Placement offset**
- Centered on hull
- Forward-offset (near the nose)
- Side-offset (asymmetric, left or right of centerline)
- Top-mounted, set back (heavy scale: bridge sits back from the bow, elevated above a forward weapons/cargo section)

---

## 4. Propulsion

**Mounting relationship**
- Bolted-on (visually distinct engine block, separate material/panel language, clear mounting seam)
- Podded (separate housing, but shaped to echo the hull's language — pylon-mounted, distinct silhouette)
- Integral (engine is a continuous extension of the hull mass itself — same panels, same material, no visible seam; the hull simply narrows/hollows into the nozzle at the rear)

**Engine count/mounting**
- Twin low-slung blocks (paired engines close to body, low profile)
- Single central engine (one large thruster on centerline)
- Distributed thrusters (multiple small thrusters spread across hull)
- Wing/pod-mounted engines (engines held out from body on short pylons)
- Cluster array (heavy scale: many mid-size engines grouped at the stern in a grid or ring, rather than one or two large ones)
- Engineering section (heavy scale: the entire aft third of the hull reads as a dedicated propulsion/reactor block, visually distinct from the forward hull)

**Visual bulk**
- Subtle (engines nearly flush, barely visible from side profile)
- Dominant (engines are the largest visual mass on the ship)
- Moderate (engines visible but proportionate to hull)

**Nozzle/exhaust style**
- Recessed nozzles (tucked into hull, minimal exposure)
- Exposed ringed nozzles (classic circular thruster bells)
- Vectoring fins (articulated exhaust with visible control surfaces)
- Glowing slit vents (thin linear exhaust glow instead of nozzles)

---

## 5. Weapons / Hardpoints

**Weapons presence**
- None (unarmed — no weapon systems at all; skip the remaining sub-categories below. Common
  for pure haulers, some explorers/scouts, and civilian racers)
- Armed (continue to the sub-categories below)

**Mounting style** *(only if armed)*
- Flush/recessed (weapons sit inside hull contours, minimal protrusion)
- External pylons (weapons hang below/beside hull on visible mounts)
- Retractable (weapon housings that imply a stowed/deployed state)

**Count & placement** *(only if armed)*
- Nose-mounted (guns clustered at the front)
- Wing/pod-mounted (spread across left/right hardpoints)
- Chin/ventral turret (mounted underneath, turret-style)
- Dorsal turret (mounted on top)

**Visual weight** *(only if armed)*
- Minimal (weapons barely register in silhouette)
- Moderate (visible but secondary to hull shape)
- Heavy (weapons/pylons dominate the profile, gunship read)

---

## 6. Appendages

**Wings/fins/stabilizers**
- None (pure hull, no appendages — reads as vacuum-only craft)
- Small stabilizer fins (functional, implies atmospheric maneuvering)
- Radiator fins (flat panels implied for heat dissipation, not aerodynamics)
- Sensor array booms (thin extended masts/antennae, not aerodynamic surfaces)

**Landing gear/struts**
- Fully retracted/hidden (clean hull, no visible gear)
- Exposed struts (visible legs, mechanical and utilitarian)
- Vehicle-style undercarriage (wheels, treads, or skids implying ground use)

---

## 7. Cargo / Utility Features
*Matters most at medium/heavy scale, but small craft can have a light version (a single rear hatch, for instance).*

**Cargo access**
- None (pure combat/racing craft, no cargo provision)
- Internal bay only (hatch/ramp not visible from outside, flush hull)
- Rear ramp/door (visible hinged or sliding cargo door, usually aft or ventral)
- External cargo pods (podded containers visibly slung under or beside the hull)
- Modular container mounts (hull has visible attachment rails/clamps for swappable cargo units)

**Docking/interface features**
- None (self-contained, no docking provision)
- Airlock/docking collar (visible circular or hexagonal docking port)
- Boarding ramp (extendable ramp/stairs for personnel, distinct from cargo ramp)
- Tractor/grapple points (visible mechanical attachment hardpoints for towing or salvage)

**Utility hardware**
- None (clean hull, no visible utility gear)
- External storage lockers (small visible compartments/boxes on hull surface)
- Mining/salvage arms (articulated mechanical arms mounted on hull)
- Sensor/communication arrays (visible dishes, booms, or antenna clusters)

---

## 8. Crew / Interior Signaling
*How much the ship "tells you" there are people inside — scales up with size class.*

**Window/viewport presence**
- None (fully opaque hull, no crew signaling)
- Cockpit/bridge only (windows limited to the primary control area)
- Scattered cabin windows (a few small windows implying crew quarters or common areas)
- Window bands (heavy scale: long horizontal window strips implying multiple internal decks)

**External walkways/access**
- None (no exterior crew-accessible surfaces implied)
- Maintenance catwalks (visible external walkway grating, usually near engines or weapons)
- Exterior ladders/handholds (implies EVA or maintenance access)
- Habitation ring/pod (heavy scale: a visibly distinct section reads as crew living space, separate from working hull sections)

**Life-support signaling**
- None (no visible external life-support hardware)
- Vents/radiators (visible heat-dissipation panels near crew sections)
- Greenhouse/observation dome (a clearly non-combat transparent structure, signals long-duration crew comfort)

---

## 9. Surface Detail Density

**Panel line density**
- Minimal/clean (few seams, smooth uninterrupted surfaces)
- Moderate (visible panel breaks at logical hull segments)
- Heavy greebling (dense mechanical detail — pipes, vents, hatches everywhere)

**Visible mechanical elements**
- None (fully smooth, sealed hull)
- Sparse (a few conduits/vents at key functional points)
- Dense (exposed pipework, mounting brackets, access hatches throughout)

**Wear/weathering**
- Pristine (factory-fresh, no visible wear)
- Lightly weathered (scuffs and panel discoloration at high-contact points)
- Heavily worn (soot streaks, scratched paint, replaced/mismatched panels)

---

## 10. Material & Color

**Base hull material read**
- Matte composite (non-reflective, flat industrial finish)
- Brushed metal (subtle metallic sheen, visible grain)
- Ceramic/ablative plating (tile-like segmented armor look)
- Polished/glossy (reflective, high-end civilian finish)

**Primary/secondary color blocking**
- Single-tone (one dominant hull color, minimal contrast)
- Two-tone split (distinct color change at a hull segment boundary)
- High-contrast panel blocking (alternating colors per panel, tactical/faction look)

**Accent elements**
- Engine glow color (blue, orange, white, green — sets propulsion "tech" read)
- Warning stripes (hazard-yellow/black accents at moving parts or vents)
- Faction/unit markings (numbers, insignia, or livery bands)

---

**How to use this:** Start with Size Class & Role (category 0) — it tells you which end of the other menus to lean toward. Then pick one value per sub-category and write it as flowing prose in the order: Size/Role → Scale & Mass → Hull → Cockpit/Bridge → Propulsion → Weapons → Appendages → Cargo/Utility → Crew Signaling → Surface Detail → Material/Color. That order mirrors how a designer's eye actually reads a vehicle — biggest structural decisions first, finish details last, with function-signaling details (cargo, crew) placed where they naturally get noticed on a real ship.

**Note on scale:** Categories 7 and 8 (Cargo/Utility, Crew Signaling) can be skipped entirely for small combat-focused craft like racers or interceptors — leave them at "None" and move on. They become much more load-bearing as you move into medium and heavy role archetypes.
